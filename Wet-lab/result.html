1. Design Overview

From candidate primers to system performance.

Respiratory infections are among the most significant diseases affecting the health of infants, young children, the elderly, and immunocompromised individuals. Respiratory syncytial virus (RSV), human metapneumovirus (hMPV), Streptococcus pneumoniae, Bordetella pertussis, and Haemophilus influenzae can all cause respiratory infections of varying severity. Traditional detection methods such as qPCR typically rely on specialized laboratory equipment, complex procedures, or lengthy culture times, which impose certain limitations in primary healthcare settings and for rapid on-site testing.

To address this issue, we developed a nucleic acid detection system for respiratory pathogens based on RPA–CRISPR/Cas12a. RPA enables rapid amplification of target nucleic acids under isothermal conditions, while Cas12a specifically recognizes the target sequence guided by crRNA and generates a detectable signal through trans-cleavage of a fluorescent reporter molecule. This system combines the specificity of target recognition with the convenience of isothermal amplification, offering a feasible solution for portable and rapid pathogen detection.

This section presents the experimental results of the following three stages in sequence, focusing on the establishment and validation of the detection system:

1. Primer screening: Compare amplification speed, endpoint fluorescence intensity, background signal, and reproducibility of different candidate primer combinations to select optimal primers suitable for detecting each pathogen;

2. System optimization: Key parameters such as reaction temperature, RNP (Cas12a:crRNA) molar ratio, RNP working concentration, and Mg²⁺ concentration were investigated through univariate experiments to determine the optimal reaction conditions for each detection system;

3. Performance evaluation: Further validate the signal performance, reproducibility, and compatibility of the detection system with portable devices or POCT settings under optimized conditions.

The target characteristics and reaction profiles of different pathogens are not entirely identical; therefore, we conducted experimental screening and condition optimization separately for each of the five pathogens. By integrating in silico design support for target sequences, primers, and crRNA with wet-lab validation, we progressively established a complete workflow encompassing sequence design, reaction system development, and detection application evaluation.

Results program 01

Respiratory Syncytial Virus

RSV
2.1 Primer Screening

I. Selection of Optimal Primer Pairs

1. Purpose

To establish an RPA amplification module suitable for RSV detection, we screened the optimal primer pairs from candidate primers F1, F2, F3, R1, R2, and R3 through orthogonal experiments. By comparing amplification peak times, end-point fluorescence intensities, amplification curve profiles, and background signals of negative controls across different primer combinations, we selected an RPA primer set with high amplification efficiency, low reaction background, and good reproducibility, laying the foundation for the subsequent development of the RPA–CRISPR/Cas12a detection system.

2. Program

1) Wipe the laminar flow hood with 75% alcohol and irradiate with UV light for 15 minutes; thaw all primers, RSV mock plasmid, EvaGreen dye, and MgSO₄ activator, briefly centrifuge, and vortex to mix thoroughly.

2) Prepare three sets of RPA reaction mixtures, each using forward primers F1, F2, and F3 paired with reverse primer R1; aliquot the reaction solutions into an 8-well strip, and include a negative control (enzyme-free water template) and a positive control (RSV plasmid template) for each set.

3) The negative reaction mixture was prepared with ddH₂O and MgSO₄; the positive reaction mixture was prepared with RSV plasmid and MgSO₄. The negative and positive mixtures were dispensed onto an eight-tube strip cap, and the capped tubes were centrifuged to ensure complete mixing of the liquids.

4) qPCR amplification program: absolute quantification, SYBR Green fluorescence channel, 40 cycles, 40°C, single cycle of 60 seconds, with continuous fluorescence signal acquisition throughout the process.

5) Export the fluorescence amplification curves and compare the peak onset times and fluorescence peak intensities of different primers.

6) The reverse primer is screened in the same manner.

3. Results

The negative control group showed a stable baseline with no non-specific amplification. All three primer sets were capable of amplifying the RSV plasmid; among them, F1R1 exhibited the earliest peak time and the highest endpoint fluorescence signal, thus F1R1 was selected as the optimal RPA primer pair for RSV (Figure 1).

II. Verify whether the PAM and gRNA are compatible with the RPA amplification product

1. Purpose

Assemble the Cas12a-gRNA (RNP) complex targeting RSV, verify whether the entire RPA-CRISPR system can specifically recognize the RPA-amplified RSV products, and confirm the availability of the gRNA and PAM site.

2. Program

1) RNP complex preparation: gRNA was mixed separately with Cas12a protein, NE Buffer, and DEPC water, incubated at 37 °C for 30 min to prepare the RNP corresponding to RSV.

2) RPA amplification system preparation: Using the optimized primers selected, prepare a batch of RPA master mix and aliquot into 8-well strips, with 7 μL per well.

3) CRISPR master mix preparation: Pre-mix RNP, FAM-reporter probe, MgSO₄, and ddH₂O, then dispense 7 μL into each well of an 8-well strip tube.

4) Template addition: Add ddH₂O to the negative control group and the RSV-specific plasmid to the positive control group; tightly close the tube caps and centrifuge to mix the reaction mixture thoroughly.

5) qPCR program: FAM fluorescence channel, 40 cycles, 40°C, single cycle of 60 seconds, with fluorescence signal acquisition.

3. Results

All negative control groups showed no fluorescence increase, with stable baseline. A clear FAM fluorescence amplification curve was generated, demonstrating that the designed gRNA can effectively recognize the corresponding RPA amplicon and that the PAM site is functional. The entire RPA-CRISPR system exhibits specificity and is ready for subsequent condition optimization.

2.2 System Optimization

2.2.1 Temperature Condition Optimization

1. Objective: To investigate the effect of reaction temperature on the fluorescent signal in RSV RPA-CRISPR detection and to determine the optimal reaction temperature.

2. Program

1) Prepare the RNP complex and incubate in the dark at 37 °C for 30 min.

2) Prepare RPA master mix in bulk; set temperature gradients: 37 °C, 37.8 °C, 38.8 °C, 41.1 °C, 42.0 °C; Each temperature gradient was set with positive (corresponding plasmid) and negative (ddH₂O) control groups.

3) Place the RPA liquid into an 8-tube row; add CRISPR mixture droplets onto the tube caps; add template and centrifuge to mix thoroughly.

4) Use a temperature gradient qPCR instrument, FAM channel, 40 cycles, single cycle 60 s, with fluorescence detection.

3. Results

The negative group showed no amplification throughout the process. The RSV fluorescent signals gradually increased with rising reaction temperature; the optimal reaction temperature for the system was determined to be 41 °C.

2.2.2 Optimization of RNP Ratio Conditions

1. Purpose

Optimize the molar ratio of gRNA to Cas12a protein to obtain a highly active RNP complex.

2. Program

1.) Set up five gradients of Cas12a:gRNA molar ratios; keep the total amount of protein and gRNA constant, adjust their volumes accordingly to prepare five sets of RNP complexes; incubate all RNPs at 37 °C for 30 min.

2) Prepare the RPA stock solution and aliquot into the bottom of an 8-tube strip.

3) Fix the Reporter probe and MgSO₄ amount, prepare CRISPR mixture using different ratios of pre-assembled RNP, and dispense into an 8-well strip tube lid.

4) Add template: ddH₂O for the negative control group and the corresponding subtype plasmid for the positive control group, then centrifuge and mix thoroughly.

5) qPCR program: FAM channel, 40 cycles, 40 °C, single cycle 60 s, fluorescence detection.

3. Results

The medium molar ratio group showed the optimal fluorescence amplification rate and peak fluorescence intensity at endpoint; excessive protein or gRNA concentration led to signal reduction; the negative control group exhibited a stable baseline. The optimal molar ratio of Cas12a to gRNA is 3:1.

2.2.3 Optimization of RNP Concentration Conditions

1. Purpose

Determine the optimal working final concentration of the RNP complex to balance detection signal and reagent consumption.

2. Program

1) Use the optimal Cas12a:gRNA ratio, perform serial dilution to obtain five RNP final concentrations; prepare the RNP and incubate at 37 °C for 30 min.

2) Prepare RPA stock solution in bulk and aliquot into the bottom of eight-tube strips.

3) Fix the Reporter probe and optimal MgSO₄ concentration, prepare CRISPR working solution with different concentrations of RNP, and dispense into an 8-well strip tube lid.

4) Add template: ddH₂O for the negative control group and plasmid for the positive control group, then centrifuge and mix thoroughly.

5) qPCR program: FAM channel, 40 cycles, 40 °C, single cycle 60 s, fluorescence detection.

3. Results

In the low to medium concentration range, the fluorescence signal increases with rising RNP concentration; however, after reaching the optimal concentration, further increases in RNP concentration lead to a decline in fluorescence signal. No amplification was observed in the negative control group. The optimal working final concentration of the RNP complex was determined to be 20 nM.

2.2.4 Optimization of Mg2+ Concentration Conditions

1. Purpose

Optimize the final concentration of MgSO₄ to enhance Cas12a trans-cleavage activity and improve the fluorescence signal intensity in detection.

2. Program

1) Prepare the RNP complex and incubate at 37 °C for 30 min.

2) Prepare RPA stock solution in bulk and aliquot into the bottom of eight-tube strips.

3) Set up a MgSO₄ concentration gradient, keep the amounts of RNP and Reporter probe constant, adjust the volume of MgSO₄, and supplement with ddH₂O to achieve the final volume. Prepare multiple sets of CRISPR working solutions and dispense them into an 8-well strip tube lid.

4) Add the template: negative control group with ddH₂O, positive control group with the corresponding plasmid, and centrifuge to mix thoroughly.

5) qPCR amplification, FAM channel, 40 cycles, single cycle 60 s, fluorescence detection.

3. Results

The fluorescent signal gradually increased with rising MgSO₄ concentration; the high MgSO₄ group showed the earliest peak, with a significantly higher fluorescence peak compared to the low-concentration group, while the negative control exhibited no background signal. The optimal final concentration of MgSO₄ in the system was determined to be 25 nM.

2.2.5 Optimization of Reporter (Probe) Concentration Conditions

1. Purpose

Optimize the final concentration of the FAM-Reporter probe to maximize fluorescence output while avoiding increased background noise.

2. Program

1) Prepare the RNP complex and incubate at 37 °C for 30 min.

2) Prepare the RPA stock solution and aliquot into the bottom of an eight-tube strip.

3) Set up a concentration gradient of the Reporter probe, prepare the CRISPR master mix; keep the amounts of RNP and MgSO₄ constant, adjust the probe amount, and add nuclease-free water to reach the final volume. Dispense the CRISPR mixture into an 8-well strip tube lid.

4) Add template: add ddH₂O to the negative group and the corresponding plasmid to the positive group, then centrifuge and mix thoroughly.

5) qPCR program: FAM channel, 40 cycles, 40 °C, single cycle 60 s, fluorescence detection.

3. Results

The fluorescent signal increased continuously with rising probe concentration; the highest probe concentration group showed the maximum Δ-fluorescence at endpoint, while the negative control baseline remained unchanged, indicating no significant background interference. The optimal final concentration of the reporter probe in the system was determined to be 2000 nM.

Results program 02

Human Metapneumovirus

hMPV
3.1 Primer Screening

I. Screening of the Optimal Primer Combination

1. Purpose

Screen the optimal RPA primer pairs for amplifying hMPV, compare the amplification performance of four primer combinations (F4R4, F4R5, F5R4, F5R5), and select candidate primers for subsequent CRISPR-based detection.

2. Program

1) Wipe the laminar flow hood with 75% alcohol and irradiate with UV light for 15 minutes; thaw primers, plasmids, and reagents at room temperature, then briefly centrifuge and vortex to mix thoroughly.

2) Prepare four sets of primer combinations and set up the RPA reaction system; include a negative control (enzyme-free water template) and a positive control (plasmid template) for each set.

3) The system was aliquoted into eight rows and subjected to RPA amplification using a real-time quantitative PCR instrument, with fluorescence signals collected.

3. Results

The negative control group showed a stable baseline with no significant amplification signal, while all positive groups exhibited amplification curves. Among the four primer sets, F4R4 produced the earliest peak and the strongest amplification signal. This step identified F4R4 as the optimal primer set for RPA performance.

II. Preliminary Validation of the Optimal Primer Combination CRISPR System

1. Purpose

Couple the best RPA primers selected with the Cas12a-gRNA CRISPR system to preliminarily validate whether the entire RPA-CRISPR system can specifically detect hMPV.

2. Program

1) Preparation of RNP complex targeting hMPV: Mix gRNA, Cas12a protein, NE Buffer, and enzyme-free water, then incubate at 37 °C for 30 min.

2) Prepare the RPA amplification working solution for F4R4 and aliquot into the bottom of an 8-tube strip.

3) Prepare a CRISPR mixture containing pre-formed RNP, FAM-reporter probe, and MgSO₄, and drop it onto the tube cap.

4) Add templates by group: add enzyme-free water to the negative control group and plasmid DNA to the positive control group; cap the tubes and centrifuge to mix all liquids thoroughly.

5) Thermal cycling program: 40 cycles, 40°C, single cycle duration of 60 seconds, fluorescence detection via FAM channel.

3. Results

The negative control group showed no fluorescence increase throughout, with a stable baseline; the positive group exhibited a significant fluorescence amplification curve. This demonstrates that the F4R4 system combined with the corresponding gRNA in the RPA-CRISPR assay possesses specific recognition capability and is suitable for subsequent condition optimization.

3.2 System Optimization

3.2.1 Temperature Condition Optimization

1. Purpose

Investigate the effect of reaction temperature on RPA-CRISPR detection signal to determine the optimal reaction temperature for the system.

2. Program

1) Prepare the RNP complex and incubate in the dark at 37 °C for 30 min.

2) Prepare RPA master mix in bulk; set up temperature gradients: 35 °C, 37 °C, 39 °C, 41 °C, and 42 °C; include positive and negative controls at each temperature.

3) Add template (negative: ddH₂O; positive: plasmid) to the RPA liquid aliquot tubes and CRISPR mixture droplet addition tube caps, respectively, then centrifuge to mix thoroughly.

4) Fluorescence PCR program: 40 cycles, single cycle of 60 seconds, reaction temperature set in gradient, fluorescence collected via the FAM channel.

3. Results

No amplification signal was detected in all negative samples, while the fluorescent signal in positive samples gradually increased with rising temperature. The fastest fluorescence peak onset and the highest Δfluorescence peak at the endpoint were observed at 42 °C (Figure 6-7). Therefore, the optimal reaction temperature for the system was determined to be 42 °C.

3.2.2 Optimization of RNP Ratio Conditions

1. Purpose

Optimize the molar ratio of Cas12a protein to gRNA to obtain the RNP complex with the highest activity.

2. Program

1) Set up a Cas12a:gRNA molar ratio gradient: 3:1, 2:1, 1:1, 1:2, 1:3; keep the total amount of protein plus gRNA constant and prepare RNP complexes accordingly; incubate all RNPs at 37 °C for 30 min.

2) Prepare and aliquot the RPA master mix uniformly; fix the amounts of probe and MgSO₄, and prepare CRISPR working solutions using different ratios of RNP.

3) Add template and centrifuge to mix; instrument program: 42 °C, 40 cycles, single cycle 60 s, FAM fluorescence detection.

3. Results

The 1:1 ratio group showed the optimal fluorescent amplification rate and highest endpoint fluorescence peak; signals were weaker in the 3:1 and 2:1 groups; while fluorescence signals significantly decreased in the 1:2 and 1:3 groups. The negative baseline remained stable (Figure 10-11). The optimal molar ratio of Cas12a to gRNA was determined to be 1:1.

3.2.3 Optimization of RNP Concentration Conditions

1. Purpose

Determine the optimal working final concentration of the RNP complex to balance detection signal and reagent consumption.

2. Program

Prepare the RNP stock solution at a 1:1 optimal ratio, then perform serial dilution to obtain final concentrations of 25 nM, 50 nM, 100 nM, 200 nM, and 300 nM; incubate at 37 °C for 30 min.

2) Prepare RPA reaction mixtures in bulk, aliquot into tube bottoms; fix the probe and add 20 mM MgSO₄, then prepare CRISPR mixtures with different concentrations of RNP.

3. Results

In the range of 25–100 nM, the fluorescent signal increased with rising RNP concentration; it reached its peak at 100 nM. Further increases to 200 nM and 300 nM resulted in a decline in signal. No amplification was observed in the negative control (Figure 12-13). The optimal final working concentration of RNP was determined to be 100 nM.

Summary of Optimal System Conditions

After multiple rounds of screening and gradient optimization, the complete set of optimal conditions for the hMPVRPA-CRISPR fluorescent detection system was obtained:

1. RPA primer set: F5R5

2. CRISPR reaction temperature: 42 °C

3. Final concentration of MgSO₄: 20 mM

4. Cas12a:gRNA molar ratio: 1:1

5. Final concentration of RNP: 100 nM

6. Final concentration of FAM Reporter probe: 2000 nM

The entire system includes negative controls to prevent non-specific amplification, enabling the detection of hMPV with high sensitivity and an excellent signal-to-noise ratio for fluorescence.

2) Prepare eight sets of RPA reaction mixtures, each using forward primers F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, F6, F7, and F8 paired with reverse primer R1. Distribute the reaction solutions into an 8-well strip, including a negative control (enzyme-free water template) and a positive control (plasmid template) for each set.
