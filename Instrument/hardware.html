Introducing ByeGerm

ByeGerm is a portable fluorescence platform developed for RT-RPA and CRISPR-Cas12a detection workflows, integrating temperature control, imaging, image processing, and record keeping on a mobile device. ByeGerm 1.0 establishes a detection and readout architecture with a fixed observation area. ByeGerm 2.0 adds a two-position sample pretreatment area and a rotating reaction chamber with fifteen tube positions to address the capacity requirements of centralized screening.

Key Technical Specifications

Development Timeline

Starting in June, we designed the enclosure and five functional modules of ByeGerm 1.0. In July, we completed module assembly, preliminary testing, and the overall circuit schematic. Software development proceeded in parallel, progressing from app functionality and interface design to integrated hardware and software testing.

After completing integration testing of the ByeGerm 1.0 modules in August, we took the device back to nursing homes and kindergartens to consult frontline staff and caregivers.

The limited number of reaction positions made it difficult to meet centralized screening needs, prompting the team to adopt a design with multiple tube positions.

To translate these requirements into an implementable structural design, we further consulted Professor Ying Mou of Zhejiang University and subsequently advanced improvements to the rotating channel, pretreatment area layout, and circuit boards. As the second-generation instrument was developed, the app was progressively adapted to image acquisition across multiple tube positions and the display of corresponding results.

3.2 Device Structure and Module Composition

ByeGerm 1.0 is compact and lightweight, allowing it to be carried by one person. It consists primarily of five internal modules (Figure 4): an optical unit for fluorescence sensing and signal amplification; a temperature control unit for precise temperature regulation; a power management unit optimized for energy efficiency; an AI data processing module that processes image data using artificial intelligence; and a Wi-Fi communication unit that connects to the mobile app for data transmission and wireless operation. The design and refinement of these modules enabled constant-temperature reagent heating over a temperature range of approximately 25–95 °C, stable fluorescence image acquisition, intelligent analysis of reaction progress and results, and data transmission[1]. The AI data processing module incorporates a high-performance Raspberry Pi single-board computer suitable for computer vision and machine learning inference.

3.2.1 Temperature Control Module

The heating platform uses two sets of high-precision ceramic heating cores with a total power of 6 W, distributed evenly around the PCR tube wells to ensure a uniform thermal field. The main controller acquires data from an 18B20 temperature sensor in real time and dynamically adjusts the PWM output through an incremental PID algorithm. This precisely regulates the power of the ceramic heating cores, maintaining temperature fluctuations within ±0.7 °C. In addition, a direct current fan with dual ball bearings operating at 3,000 rpm is integrated on the right side of the heating platform to rapidly cool the metal heating block and prevent interference from thermal inertia. The assembly of the temperature control and optical components is shown in Figure 5.

3.2.2 Optical Module and Light-Shielding Structure

A 1 W blue LED is installed beneath each PCR tube well to excite fluorescence. A 48-megapixel ultra-macro lens on a USB industrial camera module is mounted directly in front of the PCR sample wells. A bandpass filter with a center wavelength of 530 nm and a bandwidth of ±20 nm is placed in front of the lens. The filter blocks the 480 nm excitation light while transmitting green fluorescence signals at 500–550 nm, enabling clear capture of fluorescence signals. The light-shielding enclosure fixes the relative positions of the light sources, reaction tubes, and camera, reducing the effects of ambient light and repeated assembly.

After comparing five imaging distances of 70, 60, 50, 40, and 30 mm, we selected 50 mm as the fixed camera mounting distance for ByeGerm 1.0 (Figure 7).

3.2.3 Wi-Fi Communication Module

The Raspberry Pi 5 core board includes an onboard Wi-Fi module compliant with IEEE 802.11ac, supporting both the 2.4 GHz and 5 GHz bands with a maximum transmission rate of 867 Mbps. It provides relatively stable bandwidth and low-latency data transmission. A persistent connection is established using TCP/IP to transmit images, temperature data, and analysis results. The transmission interval is configurable from 100 ms to 10 s, and the average network latency is <80 ms at a distance of 10 m. A high-speed USB 3.0 interface provides a transmission bandwidth of 5 Gbps, supporting low-latency image data transfer with a single-frame transmission latency of <15 ms. Socket communication with the Android client enables recognition results to be retrieved in real time, facilitating access to and analysis of experimental data on a mobile device. Showing Boards, wiring harnesses, and support structures.

3.2.4 Image Computing Module

The Raspberry Pi uses a high-performance 2.4 GHz 64-bit ARM-based system-on-chip and incorporates the trained YOLOv8n model and an OpenCV image processing program (Figure 9). The device runs the trained YOLOv8n model to locate reaction tubes, then uses OpenCV to extract and analyze the corresponding image regions before transmitting the results to the app.

3.2.5 Power Supply Structure and Device Assembly

To achieve rapid, stable heating while maintaining a small, compact device, the power module was optimized to use a three-cell lithium battery pack (QISUO, 3,400 mAh) with a balancing and protection circuit supporting overcharge, overdischarge, overcurrent, and short-circuit protection. The intelligent power management system prioritizes power delivery to the main control unit, optical module, and Wi-Fi 6E communication module. It integrates a PD 140 W bidirectional fast-charging power bank module supporting multiple fast-charging protocols, including PD 3.1 and QC 4.0. In addition, the module uses transistors to drive a four-level LED indicator that displays the remaining battery charge in real time, allowing users to readily assess the device's remaining battery life.

We designed the enclosure and support components in SolidWorks, sliced the models, and 3D-printed them using PET[2]. During assembly, the reaction tubes, heating components, and optical components were secured first, followed by the computing and power supply components. The wiring harnesses were routed away from the camera's field of view, the fan, and the heat dissipation channels.

Further details are provided in the first-generation reproduction guide ↗.

3.3 Image Recognition and Analysis

During conventional analysis of fluorescence images using software such as ImageJ and Color Picker, different fluorescence thresholds may yield different detection results [3]. Threshold selection is also subject to variation among operators. Therefore, replacing manual image analysis with machine learning can reduce the influence of subjective factors and improve detection efficiency[4].

We use YOLOv8n to locate reaction tubes and OpenCV to extract fluorescence signals from images, then transmit the results to the mobile client (Figure 11).

Details of model training and image processing are provided on the Model page.

3.4 Mobile App Development

We developed a companion app that brings connection management, parameter settings, monitoring of reaction progress, and result recording together on a mobile phone, making instrument operation more intuitive and testing more convenient[5].

The Fluorescent detection app was developed in Java using Android Studio and is compatible with Android and subsequent operating systems. It includes four types of interfaces: Wi-Fi connection, fluorescence image acquisition and analysis, fluorescence curve display, and reaction parameter settings (Figure 12).

(A) App design architecture. (B) App interfaces, including (i) the Wi-Fi connection page, (ii) the fluorescence image acquisition and risk classification page, (iii) the fluorescence curve page, and (iv) the reaction condition settings page.

Compatibility results for the tested systems are provided in Section 6.1, and technical details are available on the Software page.

3.5 Device Operation Workflow

  1. Sample pretreatment: Extract DNA by lysis in advance and prepare the single-tube RPA-CRISPR/Cas12a reaction system.
  2. Device startup: Press the power button. The device automatically preheats, switches on the lights, and broadcasts a Wi-Fi hotspot.
  3. Sample reaction: Connect to the hotspot using the mobile app and set the reaction time and heating temperature for the specific reaction. Centrifuge the samples, place the tubes in the heating wells, and tap the corresponding control in the app to initiate fluorescence curve acquisition.
  4. Result readout: Once the reaction is complete, the detection results and the full fluorescence curve are displayed in the app.

Detailed operating instructions are provided in the ByeGerm 1.0 user manual ↗.

From ByeGerm 1.0 to ByeGerm 2.0

The first generation integrates reaction management, fluorescence readout, and app interaction into a portable device, making it more suitable for small-scale testing at home.

When the application shifts to centralized testing in kindergartens and nursing homes, each testing round must accommodate more detection reactions, controls, and replicates. The limited number of reaction positions in the first generation increases the number of separate runs required. The second generation therefore focuses on addressing insufficient throughput while also considering the hardware requirements for sample pretreatment, the arrangement of multiple reaction positions, and the association of data with those positions.

The second-generation design was developed in three directions: adding a separate lysis temperature zone to provide an internal heating position for sample pretreatment; replacing the fixed observation area with a three-face rotating reaction chamber to increase reaction capacity; and retaining the interaction framework of the first-generation app while modifying the control and acquisition programs to coordinate switching between observation faces, imaging across multiple tube positions, and record keeping on the mobile device.

ByeGerm 2.0 Detection Platform with Multiple Tube Positions

5.1 Overall Device Architecture and Main Modules

ByeGerm 2.0 measures approximately 15 × 30 × 20 cm and consists of six components: device control, AI data processing, temperature control, optics, power supply, and mechanical control. The added lysis area and rotating reaction chamber are connected through the power board, mechanical board, core board, and control board. Acquisition across multiple tube positions is managed through the existing app (Figure 14).

The front hinged lid provides access to the lysis area, and the upper hinged lid provides access to the main reaction chamber. External interfaces are grouped at the rear, and a removable battery compartment is located at the bottom (Figure 15).

ByeGerm 2.0 retains the fluorescence acquisition and mobile interaction framework of ByeGerm 1.0. The following sections describe the added structures and programs.

5.1.1 Independent Sample Pretreatment Module

The independent sample pretreatment module has two tube positions and a designed heating temperature of 90 °C (Figure 16). Because the module generates substantial heat during operation, it is housed in a separate cavity that provides physical isolation. To prevent thermal damage to the enclosure and other internal components, the space between this cavity and the main enclosure is filled with flexible polymer insulation. This creates an effective thermal barrier, ensuring structural integrity and safety during prolonged operation at high temperatures.

5.1.2 Reactions and Control across Multiple Tube Positions

The main reaction chamber is located beneath the top hinged lid and uses a rotating heating platform designed to operate at 43 °C (Figure 17). The chamber is arranged across three faces, each with five tube positions, providing fifteen reaction positions in total. As the chamber rotates, each observation face enters the fixed camera's field of view in sequence.

The PCB architecture of the device control system (Figure 18) consists of seven functional modules: the peripheral control board core module, the peripheral control board control module, the triangular rotation module, the power module, the power protection module, the charging module, and the 18650 batteries. These modules work together to provide system control, power, and charging functions.

Further details are provided in the second-generation reproduction guide ↗.

5.2 Multicolor Fluorescence Image Analysis

In this study, fluorescence images of reaction tubes from the one-step RPA-CRISPR/Cas12a system were used as the underlying dataset to develop a CNN-based fluorescence classification model. The key steps in its training process are shown in Figure 19. Details of the training methods and evaluation results are provided on the Model page.

5.3 Program Upgrades for Operation across Multiple Tube Positions

The program retains the mobile client, wireless communication, YOLOv8n, and OpenCV. After each switch between observation faces, the device captures an image containing multiple tube positions, locates each tube, and extracts its corresponding region. The app centrally manages tasks and results. The hardware and software interaction architecture is shown in Figure 20.

Further technical details are provided on the Software and Model pages.

5.4 Device Operation Workflow

Operation proceeds through connection, loading, running the device, and viewing results. Detailed operating instructions are provided in the ByeGerm 2.0 user manual ↗.

Functional Validation and Design Evaluation

6.1 Data Acquisition Compatibility across Mobile Operating Systems

To accommodate users' existing phones, we tested device connection, acquisition requests, and data display on different operating systems. The instrument acquires temperature data and fluorescence images, which the app receives wirelessly and uses to display results.

The tested devices running HarmonyOS, HyperOS, ColorOS, and OriginOS all completed data acquisition, analysis, and display, and produced consistent classification results for the test examples (Figure 22).

These conclusions apply to the phones and app versions actually tested. Continuous acquisition, reconnection, and record completeness will be included in subsequent evaluations.

6.2 Performance Results for Five Pathogen Detection Systems

Confirmation against experimental records is pending.

The five corresponding detection systems for RSV, hMPV-B, SPN, BP, and HI produced distinguishable signals for the tested targets and non-target controls.

Cost Estimation and Comparison with qPCR

8.1 Scope and Method of Cost Estimation

Costs for both generations are estimated as the sum of component and fabrication costs, assembly and testing, transportation and spare materials, and contingency, with all amounts expressed in Chinese yuan (CNY).

For ByeGerm 1.0, the existing component selection list was used, with the battery quantity adjusted to three cells. The subtotal for components and fabrication is CNY 3,573.33, and the total device budget is CNY 4,428.33. ByeGerm 2.0 retains the budget for producing one unit in a small batch, with component and fabrication costs of CNY 3,165 and a total device budget of CNY 3,980.

The estimates exclude reused terminal devices, testing consumables, early-stage research and development, certification, and after-sales services. The per-unit allocation of bulk materials and prototyping costs in the first-generation list still requires verification. Therefore, the current subtotals are not used directly to compare per-unit prices between the two generations.

This comparison is based on a prototype budget and a single commercial procurement example; it does not imply equivalent throughput, performance, or services.

The ByeGerm Story Continues

ByeGerm 1.0 was initially designed for small-scale testing at home. ByeGerm 2.0 adds an independent sample pretreatment area and a reaction chamber with multiple tube positions to address centralized testing needs in settings such as kindergartens and nursing homes. Each iteration stems from a deeper understanding of users' needs. The device still requires further refinement of operation across multiple tube positions, coordination between temperature zones, and validation of stability in real-world settings. We will continue to improve operation and result presentation based on feedback from the target users. For the next generation, we hope to explore internet connectivity and use in primary healthcare centers. Subject to privacy protection, clearly defined permissions for data use, and compliance with relevant requirements, we aim to enable secure aggregation and standardized reporting of testing records to support preliminary screening in primary care and disease control surveillance. From homes to care institutions and, ultimately, primary-level public health networks, we hope that each engineering improvement will bring testing closer to the people who need it.
