Throughout human history, respiratory pathogens have posed a persistent threat to human health. Before their causes and modes of transmission were understood, communities had limited means to identify pathogens and control outbreaks.

Conceived in 1983, PCR became a foundational technology in molecular diagnostics, enabling the amplification and detection of pathogen-specific nucleic acid sequences. However, conventional PCR workflows typically require laboratory infrastructure and trained personnel, which can limit access to rapid testing.

Important gaps remain. Delays in diagnostic results can hinder timely infection-control decisions in long-term care facilities, while crowded healthcare waiting areas can increase opportunities for respiratory pathogen transmission, including among pediatric patients.

Portable detection device was further developed to accommodate engineered RPA-CRISPR system, enabling its deployment in primary care settings.
